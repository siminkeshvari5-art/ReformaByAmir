## 1. Scope and method

- **Research question:** Which homeowners in Barcelona's upper districts (Putxet i el Farró, Gràcia, Sant Gervasi) are most reachable by a new renovation company, what makes hiring a contractor risky for them, and what could a preliminary estimator and a low-pressure consultation request credibly offer?
- **Market / geography:** Residential renovation of apartments, city of Barcelona, with a focus on the Sarrià-Sant Gervasi and Gràcia districts. Languages: ES / CA / EN.
- **Starting audience hypothesis:** private homeowners, including young homeowners, families and international residents (`Reported — founder`).
- **Website decisions supported:** test audience, core message, proof approach for a company with no Spanish track record, estimator design, and conversion experiment.
- **Method:** an Express public-signal scan of about 10 minutes. 11 sources were opened: 5 direct alternatives, 2 indirect or status-quo sources, 3 customer-language sources, and 2 category or regulatory sources, one of which is the architect firm also used as an indirect alternative. This is desk research: it gives direction and does not validate customer demand.
- **Language note:** the web-reading tool returned some fragments translated or summarised. Only fragments marked *exact* below are reproduced as they appeared in the source. The others are marked *paraphrase*.

**Category patterns (among the alternatives reviewed):**
- *Crowded:* free visit, free quote, "no surprises", "fixed price", "fulfilling deadlines". Reviews praise punctuality and detailed budgets (S5). Claiming these alone will not stand out.
- *Expected features:* itemised or downloadable budgets, portfolio photos, review counts, years in business, liability insurance, written guarantee, quick response (24–48 h).

Estimators exist (Selarom) but none reviewed pairs wide, honest ranges with an explanation of what moves the price.

## 6. Insight cards

### Card 1 — Trigger: the fear is activated by a timeline that runs out
- **Observation:** a contract for 8–9 weeks, still unfinished in October and nearly fully paid (L5–L7). A partial job left "descuidada" two months in (L1–L2).
- **Speaker and context:** Barcelona homeowners mid-project, writing to legal or professional Q&A sites.
- **Source:** S10, S8. Also S6: delays and misunderstandings are common.
- **Interpretation:** the moment of highest distrust is when the agreed date passes and the homeowner can no longer see progress or get answers.
- **Alternative interpretation:** these are outliers, since people with good experiences rarely post on complaint sites. Most Habitissimo reviews are positive (S5).
- **Evidence strength:** Directional, low–medium (three independent sources, small and partly old sample).
- **Offer implication:** a realistic written programme, progress updates at set intervals, and a defined way of handling delays.
- **Website implication:** show *how* the schedule is managed (programme, updates, what can shift it and how you'll tell the client), not a promise of on-time delivery.
- **Confirm or overturn:** 5–8 interviews with owners in the target districts who renovated in the last 3 years: "What happened when the timeline changed?"

### Card 2 — Current behaviour: owners compare several quotes and lean on intermediaries
- **Observation:** OCU advises at least three comparable, itemised quotes and notes the cheapest isn't necessarily best (S11). Platforms are built around multiple quotes (S4, S5). International residents find tradespeople through Facebook groups or use a property manager to oversee the work (S6).
- **Speaker and context:** a consumer organisation (advice); expat community readers.
- **Interpretation:** homeowners expect to compare. A quote that is easy to compare line by line is a practical advantage. International residents want someone to manage the project for them.
- **Alternative interpretation:** some owners choose by personal referral alone and never compare.
- **Evidence strength:** Directional, medium.
- **Offer implication:** itemised written quote with unit prices, materials and timeline, so it is easy to compare. "Coordination" means the owner doesn't need to hire a separate manager.
- **Website implication:** show a sample of what the quote contains. Explain that you coordinate the trades.
- **Confirm or overturn:** ask interviewees how many quotes they got and how they chose.

### Card 3 — Friction: the price grows after a vague or verbal agreement
- **Observation:** €600 agreed, €900 requested, with a verbal contract (L3–L4). In the COPE case, €36k grew to over €80k with defects (S7). OCU names hidden problems, project changes and material delays as causes of extras (S11 and search summary).
- **Interpretation:** the risk is less the starting price than an unclear scope and extras that aren't controlled.
- **Alternative interpretation:** some increases come from the owner's own changes, not the contractor.
- **Evidence strength:** Directional, medium (convergent across a Q&A site, journalism and consumer advice).
- **Offer implication:** written scope with inclusions and exclusions; any change priced and approved in writing *before* it is done; allowance for hidden conditions explained up front.
- **Website implication:** a "how changes and extras work" block; the estimator explains what makes prices move (age of installations, layout changes).
- **Confirm or overturn:** interview question: "What did the final price include that the first quote didn't?"

### Card 4 — Desired progress: things done as promised, a clean home, and not having to chase anyone
- **Observation:** the positive account stresses being on time, on budget, and daily clean-up (L8). Reviews praise punctuality, "fulfilling promises" and detailed budgets (L10).
- **Interpretation:** *functional:* the job finished as agreed. *Emotional:* not having to chase or worry. *Social:* confidence to recommend (supported only by the existence of reviews and referral practices, weak).
- **Alternative interpretation:** reviewers praise what they happened to get, not necessarily what drove their choice.
- **Evidence strength:** Directional, low–medium.
- **Offer implication:** make daily clean-up and protection of surfaces part of the written scope.
- **Website implication:** a concrete list of on-site standards (protection, daily clean-up, final clean, handover), stated as commitments you can meet, not "the cleanest".
- **Confirm or overturn:** after the first 3 projects, ask clients what they would tell a friend. Compare with these standards.

### Card 5 — Trust requirement: proof of track record, which a new firm lacks
- **Observation:** alternatives rely on 25+ years (S1, S3), 100+ projects (S2), 39–251 reviews (S3, S5), named team members, insurance numbers and 10-year guarantees (S3).
- **Interpretation:** a new company without Spanish projects competes against visible proof. Claims of reliability won't work without it.
- **Alternative interpretation:** for small partial jobs, homeowners may accept a newer firm if it is responsive, local and insured, especially through referrals. This is `Unknown`.
- **Evidence strength:** Directional, medium on what competitors use. `Unknown` on what homeowners accept.
- **Offer implication:** use proof that doesn't need a track record: a named founder you meet in person; registered S.L.; liability insurance (if held); written scope; milestone payments; documented standards; transparent status ("new in Barcelona, first projects").
- **Website implication:** honest "who you'll deal with" section with the founder, company details and what is guaranteed in writing. No fabricated reviews. Add a portfolio as real projects are completed.
- **Confirm or overturn:** measure request→visit→signed conversion for the first 20 requests, and ask people who don't proceed why.

### Card 6 — Permits confuse owners and are a common selling point
- **Observation:** Barcelona uses assabentat for cosmetic works (free, same day), comunicat immediat for layout changes (fees plus 4% of the budget, needs a technician), and comunicat diferit or a major licence for structural work (S9). OCU says to clarify who handles authorisations and community approval (S11). Competitors advertise licence management (S3, Projecte21).
- **Interpretation:** owners need to know which case applies and who is responsible.
- **Website implication:** a plain FAQ explaining the permit types, and that responsibility is agreed per project. Never say "included" by default (founder boundary).
- **Evidence strength:** Observed (regulatory summary from an architect's page; verify on the Ajuntament's site before publishing).

**Insight-quality check:** Cards 1–5 pass the specific, supported, explanatory, actionable and proportionate checks, but only with directional strength. Card 6 is regulatory context rather than an audience insight. No card claims prevalence or willingness to pay.

## 7. Price, trust, fulfilment, demand and counter-evidence signals

**Prices (with inclusions):**
- Cronoshare guide: basic €300–350/m², medium €400–700/m², high €800–1,000/m². 70 m² costs €21k–70k. Layout redistribution adds 15–25%; high-end customisation adds 15–30%. The data basis is professional bids on the platform, and whether VAT is included isn't clear (S4).
- A.B.R.: €25,625 + VAT for 70 m², itemised, with branded fixtures and kitchen appliances (S2).
- Selarom: €40,524 (50 m²) to €107,533 (190 m²) VAT included, integral package including climate control (S1).
- OCU (search summary): €60k–100k for a 100 m² integral reform in 2025 (not opened in full, so treat as a lead only).
- **Implication:** published figures differ by more than 2× for similar sizes because inclusions, VAT treatment and the flat's condition differ. The estimator must use wide ranges, state whether VAT is included, list what is included, and be calibrated with Amir's own supplier and labour costs.

**Duration:** Koncepto 10–16 weeks for premium projects (S3). A reader in S6 reported about 3 months for a piso. Lexdir contract: 8–9 weeks, overran (S10). Partial-job durations were not found (`Unknown`).

**Trust mechanisms seen:** years, project counts, review counts, named team, insurance policy numbers, written guarantees, showroom, itemised PDF budgets.

**Fulfilment signals:** contractors are often overloaded and slow to respond, sometimes ghosting people (S6). Responsiveness is a possible opening, but it can only be delivered at low volume.

**Demand signals:** paid behaviour clearly exists in the category (many firms, a price guide built from real bids, 251 reviews for one firm). **Evidence that Reform by Amir's offer would be chosen: none yet.**

**Counter-evidence and complications:**
1. "Fixed price" and "no surprises" are already standard promises (S1, S2). The founder's plan not to promise a fixed price *before inspection* is normal, not a differentiator. Competitors do give a fixed price *after* the visit.
2. Low price anchors (about €366/m² + VAT) will make fair-value pricing look expensive to visitors comparing numbers.
3. Most Habitissimo reviews are positive and punctuality is praised often. Reliable firms already exist, so "reliability" is not an unmet need in itself.
4. Spainguru readers report being satisfied with costs compared with the US. International residents may be less price-sensitive but more sensitive to communication.
5. The complaint sources are old (Habitissimo Q&A, about 12 years) or not specific to the neighbourhood.

## 8. Three website and offer implications

1. **Lead with how the project is managed, not with adjectives.** Show the written scope, the programme, the update rhythm, the change-approval rule, milestone payments and on-site standards. That is what distinguishes the offer when "fixed price" and "punctual" are everywhere.
2. **The estimator must teach rather than anchor.** Give wide ranges by finish level, say whether VAT is included, list what is included, name the factors that move the price (age of installations, layout change, permits), and follow with "the real number comes after a visit and a written scope".
3. **Earn trust honestly as a new firm.** Use a named founder, company registration, insurance status, and written commitments. Be transparent about being new in Barcelona. Invite a no-obligation visit and state the no-repeated-calls policy.

## 11. Recommended website experiment

**Hypothesis (Test decision):** owner-occupiers in the target districts who arrive from Google or Instagram will use a transparent estimator and then request a no-obligation visit when the page shows how the project is managed and what happens after they submit.

**Flow:** landing page (ES / CA / EN) → estimator (size, type, finish level → wide range plus duration plus what moves the price) → short request (description, neighbourhood, timing, name, phone or email, preferred contact method, optional estimator result) → confirmation explaining exactly what happens next and that there will be no repeated sales calls.

**Must capture:**
- traffic source and campaign (UTM), language, device;
- estimator started or completed, and inputs (type, m², finish);
- request submitted, with the fields above and consent for the chosen contact method only;
- manual follow-up log kept by the founder: contacted → visit → written quote → signed or lost, plus the reason if lost.

**Blocker:** request destination = **Connection required before launch**. Until it is connected, estimator use and clicks are not leads.

**Early reading (indicative, not a target):** judge by qualified visit requests from the target districts and the reasons given when a quote isn't accepted, not by clicks.

### Limitations
- No participant-provided customer evidence. All audience conclusions are public signals and directional.
- Small sample, some old. No Google Maps reviews or Reddit threads were reachable. Catalan-language customer sources were not found.
- Evidence for partial reforms is thinner than for integral reforms.
- The desk research cannot establish demand, willingness to pay, or whether this offer will be chosen.

### Direct-validation plan
- **Who:** 5–8 owners in Putxet, Gràcia and Sant Gervasi who renovated (partially or fully) in the last 3 years, including 2–3 international residents. Recruit via neighbours, local community groups and professional contacts.
- **Ask about past behaviour:** "Tell me about your last renovation. How did you find the contractor? How many quotes did you get, and how did you choose? What happened when something changed? What would you do differently?"
- **Riskiest assumption:** that owner-occupiers will choose a new firm offering visible management and standards over cheaper or more established options.
- **What would change the audience choice:** if requests and interviews point mostly to integral reforms of empty flats (audience A), or if price dominates every decision.
- **Behavioural test:** the website experiment in section 11, with the first 20 requests tracked to outcome.

### Open questions for `/express-offer`
1. Which services are offered first (partial jobs such as kitchens, bathrooms and flooring, versus integral reforms)? Which trades are in-house and which are subcontracted?
2. What are Amir's own costs, which determine the estimator bands and whether VAT is shown?
3. What goes into the written scope and quote, and what are the payment milestones, change-approval rule and update rhythm?
4. Liability insurance, guarantee terms, and any partner technician or architect for comunicats.
5. How to present the founder, the company and the Iran experience honestly.
6. Response-time commitment that can realistically be kept.
