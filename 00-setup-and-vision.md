## 8. Boundaries: what the website must never do

1. Position the business as the cheapest option, or use low-price claims to attract customers.
2. Make absolute or unsupported claims such as "the best", "the cheapest", or "the cleanest renovation company in Barcelona". Show standards through process, supervision and transparency, and later through real projects and client feedback.
3. Promise a final fixed price or an absolute completion date before inspecting the property and defining the scope. Realistic estimates and schedules are allowed, with the caveat that hidden conditions, client changes and material availability can affect them.
4. Imply that permits, licences, community permissions or other administrative procedures are automatically included. The site may say the business can advise or assist where applicable, with responsibility defined per project.
5. Use urgency or pressure tactics: "book now", "only a few slots left", countdown timers, temporary discounts, "offer ends this week", or similar.
6. Invent projects, reviews, testimonials, years of experience in Spain, or client numbers. This is implied by the stage and by boundary 2.
7. Suggest that economical materials mean lower workmanship standards.

Intended overall impression: calm, professional, transparent, respectful, trustworthy. Intended core messages (`Reported — founder`, to be tested): fair pricing, efficient execution, realistic scheduling, respect for agreed commitments, careful supervision, clean workmanship.
